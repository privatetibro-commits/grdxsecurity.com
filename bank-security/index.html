Bank and Credit Union Security Officers

The two most dangerous minutes in a branch are often the first and the last: opening the front door in the morning, and walking to the car after close. GRDX branch officers are posted to cover both, and everything between.

Short answer

Bank security from GRDX places armed or unarmed officers in bank and credit union branches across Northern Virginia, D.C. and Maryland. Officers support opening and closing procedures, watch the lobby and door, and deter robbery with a uniformed presence, backed by a supervisor reachable 24/7.

What does a bank security officer do?

A branch officer watches the door, the lobby and the staff during the hours money moves.

They greet customers, notice people who linger without doing business, and stand where tellers can see them. Robbers look for easy targets. A uniformed officer at the entrance makes a branch harder to pick.

  • Visible post near the entrance and teller line
  • Watching for hats, sunglasses and hoods kept on inside
  • Escorting staff to vehicles after close
  • Standing by during ATM servicing where requested
  • Incident reports to the branch manager and security officer

Opening and closing procedures

Officers follow your institution's opening and closing procedures, so the routine stays consistent no matter who is working.

At opening, the officer can check the exterior and parking area before staff go in, then wait for the all-clear signal. At close, they lock the door behind the last customer, watch the lot and walk employees out. Most branches already have written dual-control rules; the officer works inside them.

Officers also note anything that looks like surveillance: a car that idles across the street at the same hour, or a visitor who asks about the vault or shift changes.

Before opening

Exterior and lot check before staff enter.

At close

Door secured, lobby cleared, staff escorted.

Every day

Time-stamped report of anything unusual.

Armed or unarmed at the branch?

Most institutions decide branch by branch, based on location, history and policy.

A suburban branch inside a grocery store may only need an unarmed presence on Friday afternoons. A standalone branch on a busy corridor with a past robbery may want an armed officer all day. We will tell you what we would do after visiting.

Credit unions and smaller institutions

Credit unions often run lean branches with three or four employees, and that is exactly where an officer matters most.

Fewer staff means fewer eyes on the door. We can schedule officers for the busiest days, like paydays and the first of the month, instead of full-time coverage at every location.

Coverage after a robbery or threat

Right after a robbery, staff need to see someone at the door the next morning, and we can usually move fast.

Tellers who were at the window during a robbery often come back to work nervous. A visible officer for the following weeks helps with that, separate from what the investigation needs. We can also add short-term coverage when a customer makes a threat, when a former employee causes concern, or when a branch is relocating and cash is moving.

When the risk settles, coverage can scale back to peak days or opening and closing only. You decide, and the written proposal spells out each option so your security officer can compare them.

Credit union managers can also ask officers to attend staff safety briefings, so tellers know where the officer will stand and how to signal them.

Vetting, supervision and a branch assessment

Every officer is licensed and verified before scheduling, wears an inspected Class A uniform, and is checked on by a supervisor. Your security officer gets a named contact, and any officer can be replaced on request. GRDX is licensed in Virginia, D.C. and Maryland.

Call (833) 350-4739 for a branch assessment. A reply comes within one business day; we visit the branches you name and send a written proposal with officer type and hours per location.

Questions

Do banks still use security guards?+

Many do, especially standalone branches and those with a history of robbery. Some use officers only at opening, closing or peak days.

Can you cover multiple branches?+

Yes. We can staff several branches on different schedules and give you one named contact for all of them.

Will the officer follow our dual-control procedures?+

Yes. Officers are briefed on your written opening and closing procedures and work within them.

Are your bank officers armed?+

They can be. Armed and unarmed officers are both available, chosen per branch.

Can you add an officer right after a robbery?+

Yes. Tell us the branch and the hours you need, and we will confirm how quickly we can staff it. Short-term coverage can later scale down.

Do bank officers deal with check fraud?+

Fraud is handled by your staff and investigators. Officers support them by watching the lobby, documenting the person and vehicle, and stepping in if the customer becomes aggressive.
