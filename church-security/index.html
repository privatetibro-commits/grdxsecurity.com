Church Security for Services, Conferences and Revivals

Sunday morning brings hundreds of people through a handful of doors in under an hour. GRDX places licensed officers at those doors, in the parking lot and near the children's wing, working beside the safety team your congregation already has.

Short answer

Church security from GRDX means licensed armed or unarmed officers posted for worship services, conferences and revivals in Northern Virginia, D.C. and Maryland. Officers cover entrances, parking and children's areas, coordinate with your volunteer safety ministry, and report to a supervisor who is reachable around the clock.

What does a church security officer actually cover?

An officer covers the places where a congregation is most exposed: the main doors during arrival, the parking lot during the sermon, and the hallway outside children's ministry.

Most incidents at houses of worship start outside. A car break-in during the 11 o'clock service. A family dispute that follows someone from the lot to the lobby. A stranger who walks past the greeters toward the nursery. An officer in Class A uniform changes how those moments go, often before anyone inside notices.

  • Front entrance and greeter support during the arrival window
  • Parking lot and vehicle checks while the service is underway
  • Children's check-in and pickup, where custody disputes tend to surface
  • Offering count and transport to the deposit
  • Exit coverage as the building empties

How do officers work with our volunteer safety team?

They work alongside it. Your ushers and safety volunteers know the members by name, and that knowledge is hard to replace. A licensed officer adds something volunteers usually cannot: formal training, legal standing as a security professional, and a supervisor behind them.

Before the first Sunday, we walk the building with whoever leads your safety ministry. We agree on radio channels or a group text, who handles medical calls, who escorts a disruptive visitor out, and where the officer stands during prayer and altar calls. Your team keeps its roles. Ours fills the gaps.

Coverage for conferences, revivals and special services

Larger gatherings need a different plan because the crowd includes many people nobody recognizes.

A three-night revival or a regional conference can double attendance and bring in out-of-town guests, vendors and speakers. Christmas Eve and Easter bring families who visit once a year. For these, we typically add officers at overflow parking, the book table or offering area, and the green room for guest speakers.

Conferences

Registration desk, session rooms and evening sessions that run past dark.

Revivals

Multi-night coverage with the same officers each evening where possible.

Guest speakers

An escort between the vehicle, green room and platform.

Should our church choose armed or unarmed officers?

That depends on your building, your neighborhood and what your leadership is comfortable with, and we will give you a straight answer after the site walk.

Some congregations want a visible, unarmed presence that feels welcoming to first-time guests. Others, especially those with a past threat or a large open campus, choose armed officers. Some split it: armed in the lot, unarmed at the door.

Training for your ushers and safety ministry

GRDX can also train the volunteers who are there every week, so the plan holds on Sundays when an officer is not booked.

Sessions cover how to notice a person who seems out of place, how to talk someone down in a lobby, what to do in the first two minutes of a medical emergency, and how to keep children's areas locked during a lockdown. Our leadership includes a certified hostage negotiator and career law enforcement officers, so the material comes from people who have handled real calls.

How to get started

Call (833) 350-4739 or send a request, and we reply within one business day. A supervisor visits during a service or midweek, walks the lot and the hallways, and meets your safety lead. You receive a written proposal with posts, hours and officer type. If an officer is not the right fit for your congregation, we replace them on request.

Questions

Can we hire a security guard for Sunday service only?+

Yes. Many churches book officers for weekend services alone, with extra coverage for holidays and events. We set the hours around your service times, including the arrival and departure windows.

Will an armed officer make visitors uncomfortable?+

It can, which is why we talk through placement first. Armed officers are often posted in the lot or near the entrance rather than inside the sanctuary, and every officer is briefed to greet people warmly.

Do your officers handle children's check-in?+

They support it. Your children's ministry runs check-in, and the officer watches the hallway, the doors and the pickup line, where most custody problems appear.

Can you provide security for a revival or conference at another venue?+

Yes. We cover off-site events in our service areas, including hotels and rented halls. We walk the venue before the event when possible.

What areas do you serve?+

Loudoun, Fairfax, Arlington, Alexandria and Prince William in Virginia, Washington, D.C., and Montgomery, Prince George's and Baltimore in Maryland.
