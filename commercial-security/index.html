Short answer

Commercial security from GRDX provides lobby officers, armed officers, Special Police officers and vehicle patrol for offices, retail centers, warehouses and parking garages in Northern Virginia, D.C. and Maryland. Every shift produces time-stamped photo reports, and a named supervisor answers 24/7.

Which commercial properties do you cover?

We cover multi-tenant office buildings, single-tenant headquarters, retail centers, warehouses, distribution sites and parking garages.

Each has a different weak spot. Offices struggle with lobby traffic and after-hours access. Warehouses lose inventory at the dock. Garages attract break-ins and people looking for shelter. Retail centers deal with all of it plus the public.

Lobby and concierge officers

A lobby officer decides who goes upstairs. That sounds simple until 8:55 a.m., when forty people arrive at once, two couriers need signatures and a visitor insists they have a meeting.

Officers check IDs, issue visitor badges, direct deliveries and stop tailgating at turnstiles. They also become the face of the building for tenants, so we pick people who can be firm and pleasant in the same sentence.

We also cover lobby shifts on weekends, when tenants come in to catch up and the building runs with a skeleton crew.

When to use armed or Special Police officers

Armed and Special Police officers make sense where the risk is higher or where you need someone with authority to act on the property.

Special Police officers are commissioned in D.C. and Virginia and can carry legal authority on the property they protect. Clients with repeated trespass, cash handling or a known threat often choose this option. Others keep an unarmed lobby officer and add armed coverage at night.

  • Repeat trespassers who ignore an unarmed officer
  • Sites that handle cash or high-value goods
  • A specific threat against a tenant or employee
  • Late-night hours in isolated areas

Vehicle patrol for multiple buildings or large lots

Vehicle patrol covers several properties or a large campus without the cost of a fixed post at each one.

An officer drives a route between your buildings, checks doors, walks the garage and logs each stop by GPS. Portfolios with three or four small office buildings in the same county often start here. If a tenant reports a problem, the nearest patrol can be redirected.

After-hours access and loading docks

After 6 p.m., most office buildings lose their front desk, and that is when tailgating and propped doors become the main risk.

Cleaning crews, late-working tenants and contractors all need in. An after-hours officer checks badges, logs who arrives, and walks the floors before locking the perimeter. In warehouses, the same officer watches the dock as late trucks unload and confirms trailer seals and gate logs match.

Small changes add up here. A door that closes but does not latch. A stairwell exit wedged open by a smoker. Officers note those in the shift report so your engineer can fix them before they become an incident.

Questions

Do you provide security for office buildings in D.C.?+

Yes. GRDX is licensed in D.C., Virginia and Maryland and covers office buildings across the region, including Special Police officers in D.C.

What is the difference between a security guard and a Special Police officer?+

A Special Police officer is commissioned and can hold legal authority on the property they protect, which a standard guard does not. The right choice depends on your site and risk.

Can you cover just nights and weekends?+

Yes. Many commercial clients use officers or patrol only when the building is closed.

How fast can you start?+

It depends on the number of posts and officer type. After the site walk we will give you a realistic start date in the proposal.

Do you patrol parking garages?+

Yes. Garage patrol includes each level, stairwells and elevator lobbies, with logs you can review.

Can an officer manage our loading dock?+

Yes. Dock officers log trucks, check seals and paperwork against your process, and keep unauthorized people out of the warehouse.
